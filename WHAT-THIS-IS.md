# What career-kit is — and what it is not

One line: **a private, Markdown-only, evidence-traced toolkit for the whole tech career — a story bank at the center, rubrics instead of scores, options instead of advice, and you pressing every button.**

## What it is

1. **A career operating system.** One evidence spine — a story bank of what you actually did, every fact traceable to real work — that every utility consumes. The resume is an output of the spine, not the starting point.
2. **Markdown in git, local and private.** No code to run, no accounts, no backend, no telemetry. Your material never leaves your machine or your AI session. Works with any LLM: chat users paste files; agents read the repo through `AGENTS.md`.
3. **A catalog of small, sovereign utilities.** One util covers one job: check a resume, match a JD, prep a promotion case. Each util is self-contained — lift its directory out and it works, carrying its declared dependencies: the spine data and the shared material it uses. Utils are built when a real need appears, never in advance.
4. **Evidence-traced, always.** Every claim a util makes traces to something you provided. Missing facts become `[CONFIRM: ...]` placeholders, never inventions. "Not found in your material" is a valid and useful answer.
5. **Rubrics instead of scores.** The kit shows how evaluators actually judge you — level ladders, interviewer rubrics, promotion-committee thinking — and applies them with fixed labels (Strong / Weak / Absent, OK / Fix / Check). It never produces a number: no match percentage, no ATS score, no 1-to-5 rating.
6. **Options, not advice.** Every recommendation comes with its trade-offs and honest limits. You decide everything. The kit never sends, submits, publishes, or auto-fills anything on your behalf.

**Audience:** people in tech — ICs and leaders, roughly mid-level through Principal / Director. Each util narrows this further and states its own scope.

## What it is not

1. **Not an auto-apply bot.** It never submits applications, fills employer forms, or reads your mailbox. Recruiters detect automation and blacklist it.
2. **Not a scraper.** No job-board plumbing. You paste the job description; the kit does the thinking. Scraping is a maintenance treadmill and a terms-of-service risk, and the value was never in the fetching.
3. **Not a scorer.** No 1-to-5, no 0-to-100, no "80% match". Fake precision is a lie with a number on it. Counts of labeled evidence are allowed; percentages and scores are not.
4. **Not a resume builder.** No templates, no typesetting, no PDF generation. The kit judges content; layout tools (Awesome-CV, rendercv, Reactive-Resume) already exist and do that job better.
5. **Not a link list.** No outbound catalogs of other people's advice. First-party workflows, rubrics, and examples only.
6. **Not a hosted product.** No web app, no sign-up, no cloud, no analytics. Local Markdown is the privacy guarantee.
7. **Not a coach.** No pep talks, no "always negotiate", no psychological advice. Trade-offs and evidence; you choose.
8. **Not a kitchen sink.** The catalog grows only when a real need appears, never for completeness. Cut pieces — LinkedIn, job-search kit, interview prep — are candidates to return as proper utils around the spine.

## Why these lines exist

They were drawn after studying the highest-starred public repos in this space (career-ops, ai-job-search, AIHawk, Resume-Matcher, Reactive-Resume, rendercv, tech-interview-handbook, system-design-primer, and others). The winners share this kit's delivery model — plain Markdown in git, running inside the AI CLI you already own — and its full-lifecycle framing. They fail together on the same three things: they score you (fake precision), they scrape (treadmill), and their honesty is a prompt sentence instead of machinery. career-kit takes the delivery model and the honesty machinery, and refuses the rest.
