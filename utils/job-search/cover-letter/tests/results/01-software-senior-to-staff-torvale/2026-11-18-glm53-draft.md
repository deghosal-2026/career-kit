## Cover letter

Dear Hiring Manager,

Your Order Platform team is taking on the work I know best: an event-driven order pipeline and a migration off a legacy order database, with five product teams building on your APIs. I own the order management service every order at my company passes through, and last year I wrote the org-wide proposal to move us off our shared orders database — I identified the risk, laid out the migration path and cost case, and got engineering leadership's approval. Three teams are implementing it now.

The proposal came out of the work itself. Our synchronous order processing failed in bursts at peak, so I designed the event-driven order pipeline that replaced it. Earlier, I built a shared retry library and drove its adoption across teams — documentation, office hours, follow-through — until six services across four teams were using it. Both are the shape of your "What you'll do" list: setting technical direction across teams, without formal authority.

I also know what orders cost at peak. Before our checkout rewrite launched, I led a two-week load-test dry run with the payments and inventory teams, traced a race condition that dropped orders under peak load, and drove the fix. The launch had zero customer impact.

Why Torvale: event-driven order systems are the work I do best, and this role is the migration I already wrote the playbook for. I would rather shape that migration than watch it happen. I'd welcome a conversation.

Sincerely,
Jordan Avery
