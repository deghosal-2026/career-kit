# Resume best practices

Reference for the resume check. Covers the craft of a tech resume: the first quick scan, structure, role entries, bullets, skills, format, and regional norms. Level expectations live in the shared level files (`shared/levels.md` and the discipline's files); parsing rules live in `ats-rules.md`.

---

## 1. The quick scan

### What the evidence says
- **The first pass is short.** An eye-tracking study by a job site put the initial "fit or no fit" look at about 6 seconds (2012) and 7.4 seconds (2018 update). The study was small and commercial, and not peer reviewed. Other timed tests found 12 seconds to a few minutes, and a simulated hiring study found a full review took 2 to 4 minutes. Treat "about 7 seconds" as the first sort, not the whole read: a resume that passes it gets read properly.
- **What is looked at first:** name, current title and employer, current dates, previous title and employer, previous dates, then education. Bold job titles over short bullet lists drew the most attention, and a short overview at the top of page one helped.
- **What did worst:** cluttered pages, long sentences, multiple columns, little white space, and missing section or job headers.
- **Why people set a resume aside quickly** (hiring-manager surveys, 2012 and 2018, US): typos or bad grammar (the top reason in both, cited by 61% and 77%); wording copied from the job posting; an unprofessional email address; no measurable results, or tasks instead of results; long paragraphs; more than two pages; a generic resume not aimed at the role; a photo (US). Unexplained gaps are also filtered by some employers (see `ats-rules.md` section 1).
- **Weak evidence:** the scan-time figures come from one company's small studies; the surveys are US-only and several years old; "job hopping" is widely said to be a red flag, but no strong recent data was found. Use these as tendencies, not rules.
- **What the newer research adds.** The scan figures above are old but not contradicted; the picture around them has sharpened. In the current selection meta-analysis (Sackett et al., 2022), years of experience is among the weakest predictors of job performance (corrected r ≈ .07), while structured interviews and work samples are far stronger, which is why the resume is a gate, not a ranking, and why a clear, specific first pass matters more than tenure. Volume has also risen sharply (applications per role roughly doubled from 2022 to 2025, vendor-reported), and a large share of applicants now use AI to write (about 39%, Gartner 2025), so generic, samey wording is a growing risk while specific, sourced results stand out more. Treat all of this as direction, not precision.

### Quick scan checklist
Apply this to the pasted resume text before deeper review. Report each line as **OK**, **Fix**, or **Check**.

| # | Check | OK when | Fix when | Check when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Top of page one** | Name, then contact line, then a summary or the most recent role, within the first few lines. The top third states or clearly shows the **target** level, or bridges to it | The first lines are a document heading in place of the name ("Curriculum Vitae", "Resume"), an objective, a skills list or skills ratings, or long prose; or nothing in the top third signals the level | Layout can't be judged from text |
| 2 | **Role lines** | Every role shows title, employer, and month-year dates on a clear line | Any role is missing a title, employer, or dates, or dates are buried in prose | |
| 3 | **Current role** | Current title and dates are clear and match the target level | Current title is unclear, missing, or far from the target with no summary bridging it | |
| 4 | **Spelling and grammar** | No errors found | Any typo, wrong word, or repeated word. List each one | Unusual product or company spellings the user should confirm |
| 5 | **Consistency** | Same date format, tense rules, and heading style throughout | Mixed date formats, mixed tenses within a role, inconsistent headings | |
| 6 | **Dates and gaps** | Dates run in order with no overlaps that need explaining | Dates out of order, or the same dates conflict across sections | A gap of more than about six months between roles: note it neutrally, do not ask why, and offer options only if the user raises it (Shared DNA: avoid bias) |
| 7 | **Tenure pattern** | Tenures read as a coherent path | | Several roles under about a year in a row: note it neutrally; the user decides whether a short reason (contract, acquisition, layoff) belongs on the resume |
| 8 | **Walls of text** | Bullets of up to three lines that each carry one idea; white space between roles | Paragraphs of four or more lines, bullets joining several unrelated ideas, or roles with no bullets | |
| 9 | **Outcomes** | Most recent-role bullets show a result or scope | Recent bullets read as duties ("responsible for") with no result or scope | || 10 | **Length** | Within the region norm (section 7) | Over two pages, or page two holds old or filler material | Page count can't be seen in text; ask |
| 11 | **Aimed at the role** | Summary and recent bullets point at the target role | Reads as generic, or sentences are copied from the job posting | |
| 12 | **Professional details** | Plain email, current profile link, no personal details unexpected in the region. Where the region allows personal details but doesn't need them, mark **OK** and add `[OPTIONAL: remove ...]` | Unprofessional email, photo or date of birth where the region doesn't expect them, outdated links | Whether links work |

## 2. Structure
In this order:

| Section | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Header** | Name; city and country (no street address); email; phone; profile link; portfolio or code profile link if relevant. Plain text, in the body |
| **Summary** (optional, recommended from Senior up) | 2 to 3 lines. Who they are at the target level, their discipline focus, and one or two signature outcomes. No objective statement ("seeking a challenging role") |
| **Experience** | Reverse chronological. The bulk of the resume |
| **Skills** | Grouped by type. Only skills shown or usable at work. A clear skills section is expected; hiring managers do notice when one is missing |
| **Education** | Degree, institution, year (year optional). Short |
| **Optional** | Certifications, publications, talks, patents, open source, awards. Only if relevant to the target |

Remove: "References available on request", full address, objective statements, and personal details not expected in the region. Where the region allows them but doesn't need them, suggest removal as `[OPTIONAL]`, not as a Fix. Hobbies and interests are **OK** with an `[OPTIONAL]` note unless they take space the role evidence needs.

## 3. Role entries

| Practice | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Role line** | Title, employer, location (or "Remote"), dates. Make the title stand out (for example bold); it is what the eye lands on first |
| **Context line** (optional) | One line on what the team or product does and its scale, when the employer or role isn't self-explanatory |
| **Promotions** | Stack titles under one employer, each with its own dates |
| **Space by recency** | Most recent and most relevant roles get the most bullets (roughly 4 to 6; more than about 8 in one role is a Fix). Older roles get fewer (1 to 3) |
| **Older history** | Roles older than about 10 to 15 years can be collapsed into one line or an "Earlier experience" list, still with dates |
| **Leader roles** | Team size and structure in the context line or first bullet: "Led 3 teams, 22 engineers, 2 managers" |
| **Top third** | The top third of page one should already signal the target level: summary plus the most senior evidence |

## 4. Bullets

**Shape:** action verb + what they did + the result or scope. One to two lines is ideal; three is fine for a senior bullet with one idea.

| Practice | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Start with a strong, plain verb** | built, led, designed, cut, migrated, shipped, reduced, launched, hired |
| **Show the person's part** | "I" is implied. Say what they did, not only what the team did |
| **Results or scope in most bullets** | Numbers where they are meaningful and true; otherwise scope (users, teams, criticality). Not every bullet needs a number, but a recent role with none is a common reason to be set aside: and quantified results are also what an AI summary layer carries into the recruiter's view |
| **Tense** | Past tense for earlier roles. In the current role, present tense for ongoing work and past tense for finished achievements are both fine. Flag only tense that is mixed without reason, or present tense in a past role. An -ing form ("Mentoring") is not a tense. Past tense for ongoing duties mixed with present tense in the same role is a Fix |
| **No first-person pronouns** | Drop "I", "my", "we" in bullets and in the summary |
| **No filler phrases** | "Responsible for", "duties included", "worked on", "helped with", "various", "etc.". Vague intensifiers ("significantly", "greatly", "considerably") need the number: `[CONFIRM]` |
| **No unsupported soft-skill claims** | "Team player", "excellent communicator" without evidence. Show it in a bullet instead |
| **One idea per bullet** | Split bullets that join unrelated work |
| **Consistent punctuation** | Either all bullets end with a full stop or none do |
| **Avoid repetition** | The same verb or result shouldn't open several bullets in a row |

## 5. Skills

| Practice | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Group them** | For example: languages, systems and platforms, practices. Use the market's own words |
| **Evidence-backed** | Every important skill should appear somewhere in the experience section |
| **No ratings** | No stars, bars, percentages, or "expert / intermediate" scales. Bars and charts also don't survive parsing |
| **Prune** | Drop outdated or trivial items. A shorter, credible list beats a long one |

## 6. Format and language

| Practice | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Length** | Follow the region norm (section 7). More than two pages is a common reason to be set aside; every line on page two must earn its place |
| **Readable** | One font, body roughly 10 to 12 point, clear margins, white space between roles, single column |
| **Consistency** | Same date format, heading style, and bullet style throughout |
| **Spelling** | One variety of English throughout (for example US or UK), matched to the region. Proofread last; typos are the most-cited reason for quick rejection |
| **Acronyms** | Spell out uncommon acronyms once |
| **Links** | Working, written out in full, pointing to current profiles |
| **Email** | A plain, professional address |

## 7. Regional norms
Adjust format advice to the user's region. Defaults:

| Region | Name | Length | Photo | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US | Resume | 1 page (Mid), up to 2 pages (Senior and up). Some guides say 1 page at every level; 2 pages is fine when the second page carries real evidence | No | No date of birth, marital status, or photo |
| UK | CV | 2 pages | No | No date of birth or photo |
| EU | CV | 2 pages | Varies by country | Ask; some countries still expect a photo |
| India | Resume / CV | 2 pages | Usually no | Personal details sections are common but not needed |

If the region is "other", say what you are unsure of and ask. Where a region allows a detail but doesn't need it, the item is **OK** with an `[OPTIONAL: remove ...]` note, never a Fix.

## 8. Common problems by level

| Level | Common problem |
|---|---|
| **Mid** | Tasks instead of outcomes; long skills list; too long for the evidence |
| **Senior** | Ownership hidden behind "worked on"; no operational or leadership evidence |
| **Staff and Principal** | Cross-team work buried under one team; no summary signalling level; too many small bullets |
| **Manager and above** | Bullets about their own IC work; no team size; delivery lists with no people or org outcomes |
