# Software engineering Leader: Manager

## In one line
Leads one team, roughly 4 to 10 engineers. Accountable for the team's delivery, health, and growth, and still technically credible.

Tech lead managers (a small team, often 5 or fewer, plus hands-on technical leadership) fit between tracks. If the resume could be judged either way, ask which level the user is aiming for and judge against that one.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Team size and charter stated, with direct reports or formal accountability for the team. *"Managed 8 engineers owning the checkout service."* | *"Led a team"* with no size or charter; a tech lead role without reports | Only IC bullets |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the team roadmap from business goals; negotiated priorities with product; balanced new work against maintenance and technical debt | Delivered a roadmap handed down | No sign of shaping the work |
| **Depth** | Made or guided key technical calls; credible in design reviews; set quality and operational standards | A list of technologies | No technical credibility shown. A concern for an engineering manager |
| **Influence** | Partner teams or product changed plans because of them; represented the team in planning | *"Worked closely with product"* | Own team only |
| **People** | Hires, promotions, performance handled (including underperformance), retention; grew the team from X to Y; sponsored people into bigger roles | *"Mentored the team"* | None. A blocker for Manager |
| **Business impact** | Team outcomes with numbers: delivery predictability, incidents, a business metric; team health such as attrition, engagement, or on-call load | Lists projects delivered | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Depth, Ambiguity, and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Bullets describe their own coding, not the team's results. Code reviews and on-call cover are fine; owning major features on the critical path is a warning sign.
- No team size anywhere.
- "I built" everywhere, "the team" nowhere.
- No hiring or growing people.
- Manager title, but the evidence shows tech lead scope without reports.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the payments team. | Managed 7 engineers owning payments processing ($400M a year); grew the team from 4 and hired 5, with no regretted attrition in 2 years. |
| Improved team delivery. | Introduced quarterly planning and a weekly risk review; on-time delivery of committed work rose from about 50% to 85%. |
| Mentored engineers. | Promoted 3 engineers (2 to Senior, 1 to Staff) through written growth plans and stretch projects. |

## What separates Manager from Senior Manager
A Senior Manager resume shows:
- Several teams, or managers and tech leads reporting to them.
- Charters set for several teams.
- Headcount planning and hiring plans.
- Cross-team programs delivered.
- Growing managers and tech leads.
- Outcomes across teams, not one team.
