# Software engineering IC: Principal

Top of the IC track in career-kit. Levels above Principal are out of scope.

The Staff shapes (tech lead, architect, solver, right hand; see `staff.md`) apply here too, at org or company scale.

## In one line
Owns technical direction for a domain across the org or company over years. Shapes leadership decisions and is the recognised authority for the domain across the company, often outside it too.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | A domain across the org or company. *"Defined the multi-region architecture used by all 40 product teams."* | Several teams within one org | One area or team |
| **Ambiguity** | Set multi-year technical strategy; reframed what the company should build | Executed someone else's strategy at large scale | Delivers defined projects |
| **Depth** | Recognised authority: the person the company turns to for the domain; final reviewer for critical designs; owns standards others must follow. External signals (talks, papers, patents, major open source) strengthen the case but are not required | Deep, but recognised only inside one area | No authority signal |
| **Influence** | Changed leadership decisions: platform bets, build vs buy, org priorities; co-defines org strategy with directors and executives | Influence across teams within one org | Own area only |
| **People** | Grew and sponsored Staff engineers; shaped the IC career ladder, hiring bar, or engineering culture; takes part in senior hiring and calibration | Mentors Seniors | None |
| **Business impact** | Company-level outcomes: cost base, risk, new business capability, tied to revenue or major risk; product opportunities their technical work made possible | Org-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. People may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a strong Staff engineer with a long tenure.
- Outcomes confined to one org.
- No evidence of executive-level decisions.
- A long list of projects instead of a few big bets.
- External visibility claimed but not shown (no talk, paper, patent, or project named).

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Architect for the platform. | Set the 3-year architecture for the company's move to multi-region; the CTO's staff adopted the plan, and it now underpins all 40 product teams. |
| Spoke at conferences. | Gave 3 talks at major industry conferences on event-driven payments; the open-source library from that work is used by 2 of our partners. |
| Reduced infrastructure costs. | Led the build-versus-buy decision for observability and presented it to the exec team; the move saves $2.4M a year. |

## What holds people back at Principal
- No evidence of company-level decisions.
- Impact confined to one org.
- Too many small projects, not enough big bets.
- Authority claimed but not shown: no named decisions, reviews, or standards they own.
- The resume never says why the work mattered to the business.
