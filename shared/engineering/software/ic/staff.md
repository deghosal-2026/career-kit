# Software engineering IC: Staff

## In one line
Sets technical direction for an area spanning several teams, or solves the problems that matter most to the org. Finds and frames problems nobody assigned, and leads through influence rather than authority.

## Shapes of Staff
Staff work comes in several common shapes (after Will Larson's *Staff Engineer* archetypes). All are valid. Judge each by its own evidence; do not mark someone down for not being a different shape.

| Shape | What it looks like | Where Scope comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Tech lead** | Guides the direction and delivery of a cluster of teams, or of one large, critical team | Number of teams and the area's importance |
| **Architect** | Owns direction and quality for a technical domain | The domain's reach across the org |
| **Solver** | Goes deep on the hardest problems, one after another | The importance of the problems, not the number of teams |
| **Right hand** | Extends a senior leader's reach across an org | The leader's org |

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | An area across several teams. *"Led the move of 14 services across 5 teams off the shared orders database."* | A large system within one team | One team's features |
| **Ambiguity** | Spotted an unassigned problem, built the case, and got it funded or prioritised; or explored a risky area and brought back findings that changed the plan, a well-argued "don't build this" included | Led a cross-team project someone else defined | Executes assigned work |
| **Depth** | Wrote architecture docs or RFCs that became standards; reviews designs across teams | Deep in one system only | No design authorship |
| **Influence** | Several teams changed their approach because of them; aligned teams with conflicting goals; brought the engineering view into a leadership decision and changed it | *"Collaborated with other teams"* | Only own team. A blocker for Staff |
| **People** | Grew and sponsored Senior engineers (promotions, people put forward to lead projects); started forums like design review; raises the hiring bar | Mentors juniors only | None |
| **Business impact** | Org-level outcomes: cost saved, reliability across a product, a roadmap unblocked | One system's metric | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, Ambiguity, and Influence Strong. For the Solver shape, Scope can be Strong through the importance of the problems solved even when few teams are involved. Depth and Business impact Strong, or Weak with a clear story. People at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Every bullet sits inside one team's system.
- Cross-team work described with *helped, supported, contributed to*.
- No written direction: no RFCs, strategy docs, or standards.
- Outcomes measured only at system level.
- "Tech Lead" title with single-team scope and no influence beyond that team.
- Many small, visible tasks and no hard, important problem seen through to the end.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Worked with several teams on the database migration. | Proposed and led the move off the shared orders database across 5 teams; wrote the migration RFC, adopted as the org standard, and finished in 3 quarters with no customer-facing downtime. |
| Improved the build pipeline for the org. | Found flaky tests were costing about 300 engineer-hours a month; built the case, got 3 teams to adopt a quarantine system, and cut flaky failures by 70%. |
| Mentored engineers. | Mentored 4 Senior engineers, 2 since promoted to Staff. Started the cross-team design review that now covers every new service. |

## What separates Staff from Principal
A Principal resume shows:
- A domain across the org or company, not one area.
- Multi-year direction for a whole domain, co-owned with senior leadership, not a one- to two-year plan for one area.
- Influence on leadership decisions: platform bets, build vs buy, org priorities.
- Recognition outside their team and often outside the company: talks, papers, patents, major open source.
- Growing Staff engineers.
- Outcomes that change the company's cost base, risk, or capabilities.
