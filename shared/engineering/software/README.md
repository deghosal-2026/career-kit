# Software engineering rubric

Covers backend, frontend, mobile, and full-stack engineering. They share one rubric: the level signals are the same, only the technologies differ.

## Notes for the evaluator
- **Backend, frontend, mobile, and full-stack are judged equally.** "System" means a backend service, an area of a client app, or a shared library. Client roles show operational ownership through release quality and crash or error rates, not on-call.
- **Engineering metrics count as business impact.** Latency, availability, cost, build and release speed, and defect rates are valid outcomes. Many engineers never see revenue figures.
- **Not every bullet needs a number.** Scope (users, traffic, teams, criticality) can carry a bullet. Do not push `[CONFIRM]` for a number on every line; ask for numbers only where they would change how the bullet reads.
- **Years of experience are not a level signal.** Judge scope and impact. Long tenure at one level does not make someone the next level.
- **Written communication counts.** From Senior up, design docs, RFCs, and strategy docs are evidence under Depth and Influence.
- **Glue work counts when it is written as leadership.** Unblocking other teams, aligning people, reviewing designs, onboarding, and seeing cross-team projects through to the end are real evidence from Senior up, often the main evidence at Staff. Mark them Weak when written as *helped* or *supported*, and Strong when written as an outcome the person led.
- **Sponsoring beats mentoring.** From Staff up (and for managers at every level), putting people forward for bigger scope, such as naming them to lead a project or backing a promotion, is stronger evidence than giving advice.

- **Reach decides the level, not the size of the number.** Apply the outside-observer test (`../../levels.md` §6–7): a Senior outcome gets repeated in a team or product review; a Staff outcome in an engineering-leadership meeting; a Principal or Director outcome in front of company leadership or on an external stage. Judge who benefits and how widely, not the percentage.
- **Levels are awarded conservatively.** Promotion lags demonstrated performance, the next level's scope must already be shown, consistently, over time. One level-sized project inside a year of lower-level work reads as the lower level with a highlight, not as the next level. See `../../levels.md` §8.
