# Platform / Infra / DevOps engineering IC: Principal

Top of the IC track in career-kit. Levels above Principal are out of scope.

The Staff shapes (tech lead, architect, solver, right hand; see `staff.md`) apply here too, at org or company scale.

## In one line
Owns technical direction for how the company builds, ships, and runs software over years. Shapes leadership decisions on platform and infrastructure investment and is a recognised authority inside and outside the company.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | A domain across the org or company. *"Defined the platform architecture that all 90 engineering teams build and ship on."* | Several teams within one org | One area or team |
| **Ambiguity** | Set multi-year platform or infrastructure strategy; reframed what the company should build itself and what it should buy | Executed someone else's strategy at large scale | Delivers defined projects |
| **Depth** | Recognised authority: the person the company turns to for infrastructure and delivery decisions; final reviewer for critical designs; owns standards others must follow. External signals (talks, papers, patents, major open source) strengthen the case but are not required | Deep, but recognised only inside one area | No authority signal |
| **Influence** | Changed leadership decisions: hosting strategy, build vs buy, how much to invest in the platform, org priorities; works with executives | Influence across teams within one org | Own area only |
| **People** | Grew Staff engineers; shaped the IC career ladder, hiring bar, or engineering culture | Mentors Seniors | None |
| **Business impact** | Company-level outcomes: infrastructure cost base, engineering speed across the company, risk, a new business capability such as entering a new region | Org-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. People may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a strong Staff engineer with a long tenure.
- Outcomes confined to one org.
- No evidence of executive-level decisions.
- A long list of tools and projects instead of a few big bets.
- Platform described by what it contains, not by what it changed for the company.
- External visibility claimed but not shown (no talk, paper, patent, or project named).

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Architect for the infrastructure platform. | Set the 3-year platform strategy that moved 90 teams from team-run infrastructure to a shared self-service platform; the CTO's staff adopted it, and new services now reach production in 2 days instead of 6 weeks. |
| Spoke at conferences. | Gave 3 talks at major industry conferences on running large-scale build systems; the open-source build caching project from that work is used by several outside companies. |
| Reduced infrastructure costs. | Led the decision on how the company hosts its workloads and presented it to the exec team; the change cut infrastructure cost from 18% to 11% of revenue over 2 years. |

## What holds people back at Principal
- No evidence of company-level decisions.
- Impact confined to one org.
- Too many small projects, not enough big bets.
- Authority claimed but not shown: no named decisions, reviews, or standards they own.
- The resume never says why the platform mattered to the business: cost, speed, risk, or new capability.
