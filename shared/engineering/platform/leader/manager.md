# Platform / Infra / DevOps engineering Leader: Manager

## In one line
Leads one platform or infrastructure team, roughly 4 to 10 engineers. Accountable for the team's delivery, health, and growth, for the experience of the internal teams it serves, and still technically credible.

Tech lead managers (a small team plus hands-on technical leadership) fit between tracks. If the resume could be judged either way, ask which level the user is aiming for and judge against that one.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Team size, charter, and users stated. *"Managed 7 engineers owning build and deploy for 400 developers."* | *"Led a team"* with no size, charter, or users | Only IC bullets |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the team roadmap from user needs and business goals; turned a request queue into a planned roadmap | Delivered a roadmap handed down; worked the request queue | No sign of shaping the work |
| **Depth** | Made or guided key technical calls; credible in design reviews; set standards for how the platform is changed and run | A list of tools | No technical credibility shown. A concern for an engineering manager |
| **Influence** | User teams or product leadership changed plans because of them; ran user feedback and adoption reviews; represented the team in planning | *"Worked closely with development teams"* | Own team only |
| **People** | Hires, promotions, performance handled, retention; grew the team from X to Y | *"Mentored the team"* | None. A blocker for Manager |
| **Business impact** | Team outcomes with numbers: adoption, delivery speed for user teams, cost, developer satisfaction; team health such as attrition, engagement, or interrupt load | Lists projects delivered | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Depth, Ambiguity, and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Bullets describe their own hands-on infrastructure work, not the team's results.
- No team size anywhere, and no sense of who uses what the team builds.
- The team is described as a service desk: tickets closed, requests handled.
- "I built" everywhere, "the team" nowhere.
- No hiring or growing people.
- Manager title, but the evidence shows tech lead scope without reports.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the DevOps team. | Managed 7 engineers owning build and deploy for 400 developers; grew the team from 4 and hired 4, with no regretted attrition in 2 years. |
| Improved how the team handled requests. | Cut the team's request queue from about 120 tickets a month to 25 by making the 5 most common requests self-service; freed half the team's time for roadmap work. |
| Ran developer surveys. | Started a quarterly survey of the 400 developers the team serves and planned the roadmap from it; satisfaction with the deploy process rose from 52% to 81% in a year. |

## What separates Manager from Senior Manager
A Senior Manager resume shows:
- Several platform or infrastructure teams, or managers and tech leads reporting to them.
- Charters set for several teams, with clear lines between what each team owns.
- Headcount planning and hiring plans.
- Cross-team programs delivered, such as an org-wide migration.
- Growing managers and tech leads.
- Outcomes across teams and for the wider engineering org, not one team.
