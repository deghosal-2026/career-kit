# SRE Leader: Manager

## In one line
Leads one SRE team, roughly 4 to 10 engineers. Accountable for the reliability of the services the team supports, the health of its on-call, and the growth of its people, and still technically credible.

Tech lead managers (a small team plus hands-on reliability leadership) fit between tracks. If the resume could be judged either way, ask which level the user is aiming for and judge against that one.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Team size and what it supports stated. *"Managed 7 SREs supporting the 12 services behind payments."* | *"Led the SRE team"* with no size or services | Only IC bullets |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the team's reliability roadmap from SLOs and incident data; agreed the engagement model with product teams; said no to work outside the charter | Delivered a roadmap handed down | No sign of shaping the work |
| **Depth** | Credible in incident reviews and design reviews; set alerting, review, and readiness standards for the team | A list of tools | No technical credibility shown. A concern for an SRE manager |
| **Influence** | Got product teams to fund reliability work or honour an error budget policy; represented reliability in planning | *"Worked closely with development teams"* | Own team only |
| **People** | Hires, promotions, performance handled, retention; grew the team; built an on-call rotation people can sustain | *"Mentored the team"* | None. A blocker for Manager |
| **Business impact** | Reliability outcomes with numbers (SLO attainment, downtime, repeat incidents) and on-call health (pages per shift, out-of-hours pages, toil share, attrition) | Lists projects delivered or incidents handled | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Depth, Ambiguity, and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Bullets describe their own incident work, not the team's results.
- No team size or supported services anywhere.
- The team is described as a ticket queue for other teams, with no charter or limit on toil.
- On-call health never mentioned.
- No hiring or growing people.
- Manager title, but the evidence shows a senior on-call lead without reports.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the SRE team. | Managed 7 SREs supporting 12 payment services; grew the team from 4 and hired 4, with no regretted attrition in 2 years. |
| Improved on-call for the team. | Cut pages from 18 to 4 a week and out-of-hours pages by 75% by funding alert cleanup and capping toil at a third of team time; on-call volunteers for the follow-the-sun rotation rose from 3 to 8. |
| Worked with development teams on reliability. | Agreed error budget policies with 4 product teams; budget breaches now trigger reliability sprints, and quarters within SLO rose from 5 of 12 to 11 of 12 across the services. |

## What separates Manager from Senior Manager
A Senior Manager resume shows:
- Several SRE teams, or managers and tech leads reporting to them.
- Charters and engagement models set for several teams.
- Headcount planning: how much SRE support each product area gets.
- Cross-team reliability programs delivered.
- Growing managers and tech leads.
- Reliability outcomes across a product area, not one team's services.
