# SRE Leader: Senior Manager

## In one line
Leads several SRE teams, often through managers or tech leads, roughly 15 to 40 people. Sets team charters and engagement models, decides where SRE support goes, and delivers reliability across a product area.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Several teams with structure stated, plus what they support. *"Led 3 SRE teams (24 engineers, 2 managers) supporting 60 services across commerce."* | One large team, no structure | One team |
| **Ambiguity** | Defined charters and the engagement model (embedded, central, or consulting); decided which services get SRE support and which go back to their owners; turned business goals into a multi-team reliability roadmap | Managed teams with charters already set | No shaping |
| **Depth** | Arbitrated cross-team reliability trade-offs; set reliability architecture direction with Staff engineers; owned the incident process for the area | Team-level technical calls | No technical credibility shown |
| **Influence** | Shaped org planning so reliability work is funded; partnered with product and engineering leaders on error budget policy; changed priorities across the org | Influence within own teams only | None shown |
| **People** | Grew managers (first-time managers developed); built the hiring pipeline; balanced on-call load and seniority across teams or sites; planned succession | Hired ICs only | None |
| **Business impact** | Reliability outcomes across a product area tied to business goals: downtime, SLO attainment, cost of capacity; owned a slice of headcount or budget | Team-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Ambiguity ideally Strong. Depth and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a one-team SRE manager scaled up.
- No managers or tech leads reporting to them.
- No planning, headcount, or decisions about where SRE support goes.
- Outcomes listed as incidents handled or tools shipped, not results.
- Teams take on every service asked of them, with no charter or limit.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed multiple SRE teams. | Led 3 SRE teams (24 engineers, 2 managers) supporting 60 commerce services; set a shared engagement model and a reliability roadmap tied to the peak sales season. |
| Hired a lot of SREs. | Built the hiring plan for 10 roles across 2 sites and filled 9; developed 2 tech leads into first-time managers and balanced on-call so neither site took nights. |
| Improved reliability across commerce. | Ran a 3-team program to make checkout, cart, and payments survive a zone loss; peak-season downtime fell from 3.5 hours to 20 minutes year over year, and spare capacity cost fell 25%. |

## What separates Senior Manager from Director
A Director resume shows:
- An org of several teams and managers, often 25 to 100+ people.
- The org's reliability strategy and structure, not only its delivery.
- Budget ownership, where the company gives Directors a budget.
- A leadership bench: managers of managers, succession.
- Partnership with executives on reliability targets and risk.
- Outcomes tied to company goals.
