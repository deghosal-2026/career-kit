# SRE Leader: Director

Top of the Leader track in career-kit. VP and above are out of scope.

## In one line
Leads an SRE org of several teams and managers, often 25 to 100+ people. Owns the company's reliability strategy, incident practice, org design, and budget, and partners with executives on reliability targets and risk.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Org size and structure stated, plus what it supports and budget where they own one. *"55 SREs across 6 teams and 5 managers, supporting 400 engineers."* | Several teams, no managers or org shape | One or two teams |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the org's multi-year reliability strategy; chose the SRE model for the company and the ratio of SREs to product engineers; took SRE into a new area | Executes a strategy set by leaders above | No shaping |
| **Depth** | Set the reliability bar for the company: SLO framework, incident and review practice, readiness criteria; major resilience bets with outcomes | *"Drove reliability excellence"* | None shown |
| **Influence** | Shaped company planning so reliability is funded as a core cost; agreed customer-facing reliability targets with executives; led cross-org initiatives | Influence within own org only | None shown |
| **People** | Built a leadership bench; manages managers; succession; on-call health and attrition data across the org; org design | Hiring counts only | None |
| **Business impact** | Org outcomes tied to company goals: major-outage risk, contractual commitments met, cost of downtime, infrastructure cost; budget figures | A list of projects delivered | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. Depth may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Delivery bullets that read like a Senior Manager.
- No org size, supported scope, or budget.
- No reliability strategy or org design.
- No evidence of developing leaders.
- Impact described as incidents handled or tools shipped.
- Startup "Head of SRE" with Manager scope.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Led site reliability engineering. | Led SRE (55 engineers, 6 teams, 5 managers, $9M budget) supporting 400 product engineers; set the 2-year strategy that moved the company from a central ops queue to embedded SREs with shared SLOs. |
| Improved the incident culture. | Introduced a company-wide incident and review practice across 45 teams; customer-facing major incidents fell from 26 to 9 a year, and on-call attrition dropped from 22% to 8%. |
| Worked with executives on reliability. | Partnered with the CTO and sales leadership to set the first contractual availability target; met it for 6 straight quarters, cutting service credits paid from $1.4M to under $100k a year. |

## What holds people back at Director
- Impact not tied to company goals.
- No org size, or no budget figures when they do own a budget. Not every company gives Directors a budget.
- Reads like a Senior Manager with a bigger team.
- No evidence of reliability strategy or org design decisions.
- Reliability presented as firefighting, not as a funded, planned investment.
