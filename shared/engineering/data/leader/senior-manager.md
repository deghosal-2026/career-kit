# Data engineering Leader: Senior Manager

## In one line
Leads several data teams, often through managers or tech leads, roughly 15 to 40 people. Sets charters, plans headcount and spend, and delivers data outcomes across teams.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Several teams with structure stated. *"Led 3 teams (24 engineers, 2 managers) owning ingestion, the data platform, and core models."* | One large team, no structure | One team |
| **Ambiguity** | Defined team charters; reorganised teams around producers, platform, or consumers; turned company data needs into a multi-team roadmap | Managed teams with charters already set | No shaping |
| **Depth** | Arbitrated cross-team data trade-offs: freshness vs cost, central vs team-owned models; set architecture direction with Staff engineers | Team-level technical calls | No technical credibility shown |
| **Influence** | Shaped org planning; partnered with analytics, ML, product, security, and legal leaders; changed how other orgs produce or use data | Influence within own teams only | None shown |
| **People** | Grew managers (first-time managers developed); built the hiring pipeline; planned succession | Hired ICs only | None |
| **Business impact** | Outcomes across teams tied to business goals: platform adoption, data spend, audit or compliance results; owned a slice of headcount or budget, often including compute and vendor spend | Team-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Ambiguity ideally Strong. Depth and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a one-team manager scaled up.
- No managers or tech leads reporting to them.
- No planning, headcount, spend, or hiring plans.
- Outcomes listed as migrations or platforms launched, not results.
- Governance or privacy work mentioned only as "ensured compliance", with no outcome.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed multiple data teams. | Led 3 teams (24 engineers, 2 managers) across ingestion, platform, and core models; set charters and a shared roadmap tied to the company's reporting and ML goals. |
| Owned the data platform budget. | Owned $2.8M a year in data compute and vendor spend; introduced per-team cost reporting and cut spend per TB processed by 35% while volume grew 60%. |
| Delivered the new data platform. | Delivered the self-serve data platform across 3 teams on schedule; 14 product teams now publish their own datasets, and time to a new dataset fell from 6 weeks to 4 days. |

## What separates Senior Manager from Director
A Director resume shows:
- An org of several data teams and managers, often 25 to 100+ people.
- The org's data strategy and structure, not only its delivery.
- Budget ownership, where the company gives Directors a budget, often including data compute, storage, and vendor contracts.
- A leadership bench: managers of managers, succession.
- Partnership with executives on data, cost, and risk.
- Outcomes tied to company goals.
