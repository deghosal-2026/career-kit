# Data engineering Leader: Manager

## In one line
Leads one data engineering team, roughly 4 to 10 engineers. Accountable for the team's delivery, data reliability, health, and growth, and still technically credible.

Tech lead managers (a small team plus hands-on technical leadership) fit between tracks. If the resume could be judged either way, ask which level the user is aiming for and judge against that one.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Team size and charter stated. *"Managed 6 data engineers owning ingestion and the core warehouse models."* | *"Led the data team"* with no size or charter | Only IC bullets |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the team roadmap from business and consumer needs; negotiated priorities with analytics, ML, and product teams; cut ad hoc request load | Delivered a roadmap handed down | No sign of shaping the work |
| **Depth** | Made or guided key data design calls; credible in design reviews; set quality, freshness, and on-call standards | A list of technologies | No technical credibility shown. A concern for a data engineering manager |
| **Influence** | Producer or consumer teams changed plans because of them; agreed data targets with partner teams; represented the team in planning | *"Worked closely with stakeholders"* | Own team only |
| **People** | Hires, promotions, performance handled, retention; grew the team from X to Y | *"Mentored the team"* | None. A blocker for Manager |
| **Business impact** | Team outcomes with numbers: freshness targets met, data incidents, compute spend, delivery predictability, consumer satisfaction; team health such as attrition or on-call load | Lists pipelines or projects delivered | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Depth, Ambiguity, and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Bullets describe their own pipeline work, not the team's results.
- No team size anywhere.
- "I built" everywhere, "the team" nowhere.
- No hiring or growing people.
- The team is described as a ticket queue for other teams, with no roadmap of its own.
- Manager title, but the evidence shows tech lead scope without reports.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the data engineering team. | Managed 6 data engineers owning ingestion and core warehouse models used by 20 teams; grew the team from 3 and hired 4, with no regretted attrition in 2 years. |
| Improved data reliability. | Introduced freshness targets, a weekly data incident review, and an on-call rotation; days with late finance data fell from 11 a month to 1, and after-hours pages halved. |
| Handled requests from analytics teams. | Replaced an ad hoc request queue with a quarterly roadmap agreed with 4 consuming teams; request backlog fell from 140 to 25, and 3 teams moved to self-serve models. |

## What separates Manager from Senior Manager
A Senior Manager resume shows:
- Several data teams, or managers and tech leads reporting to them.
- Charters set for several teams: for example ingestion, platform, and modelling.
- Headcount planning, hiring plans, and often a share of data compute or vendor spend.
- Cross-team programs delivered, such as a platform migration or a governance rollout.
- Growing managers and tech leads.
- Outcomes across teams, not one team.
