# Data engineering IC: Principal

Top of the IC track in career-kit. Levels above Principal are out of scope.

The Staff shapes (tech lead, architect, solver, right hand; see `staff.md`) apply here too, at org or company scale.

## In one line
Owns data direction for a domain across the org or company over years: how data is produced, stored, governed, and paid for. Shapes leadership decisions and is a recognised authority inside and outside the company.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | A data domain across the org or company. *"Defined the data architecture and ownership model used by all 35 product teams."* | Several teams within one org | One area or team |
| **Ambiguity** | Set multi-year data strategy; reframed how the company should own, govern, or invest in data | Executed someone else's data strategy at large scale | Delivers defined projects |
| **Depth** | Recognised authority: the person the company turns to on data architecture; final reviewer for critical data designs; owns standards others must follow, such as modelling, contracts, or access policy. External signals (talks, papers, patents, major open source) strengthen the case but are not required | Deep, but recognised only inside one area | No authority signal |
| **Influence** | Changed leadership decisions: platform bets, build vs buy, central vs domain-owned data, privacy and retention policy; works with executives, legal, and security leaders | Influence across teams within one org | Own area only |
| **People** | Grew Staff engineers; shaped the data engineering career ladder, hiring bar, or data culture across the company | Mentors Seniors | None |
| **Business impact** | Company-level outcomes: data cost base, regulatory risk, a new data-driven business capability, tied to revenue or major risk | Org-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. People may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a strong Staff engineer with a long tenure.
- Outcomes confined to one org or one platform.
- No evidence of executive-level decisions.
- A long list of migrations and pipelines instead of a few big bets.
- External visibility claimed but not shown (no talk, paper, patent, or project named).

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Data architect for the company. | Set the 3-year plan to move from one central data team to domain-owned data products on a shared platform; the CTO's staff adopted it, and 35 product teams now publish data under one set of standards. |
| Led data privacy work. | Designed the company's model for personal data: tagging, access tiers, and automated deletion across 4,000 tables; deletion requests went from 30 days of manual work to 48 hours, and the next regulator audit had no findings. |
| Reduced data infrastructure costs. | Led the build-versus-buy review for the data platform and presented it to the exec team; the chosen path cut annual data spend by $3.2M while doubling processed volume. |

## What holds people back at Principal
- No evidence of company-level decisions about data.
- Impact confined to one org or one platform.
- Too many migrations and pipelines, not enough big bets.
- Authority claimed but not shown: no named decisions, reviews, or standards they own.
- The resume never says why the data work mattered to the business: cost, risk, or what the company could now do.
