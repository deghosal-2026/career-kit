# Security engineering Leader: Director

Top of the Leader track in career-kit. VP, CISO, and above are out of scope. A "Head of Security" who owns the whole security function, including governance and compliance, and reports to the CEO is closer to a CISO; judge only the engineering leadership against this file.

## In one line
Leads a security engineering org of several teams and managers, often 25 to 100+ people. Owns strategy, org design, and budget, and partners with executives on risk.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Org size and structure stated, plus budget where they own one. *"45 engineers across 5 teams and 5 managers."* | Several teams, no managers or org shape | One or two teams |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the org's multi-year security strategy; reorganised the org; took it into a new area | Executes a strategy set by leaders above | No shaping |
| **Depth** | Set org-wide security architecture principles or an org-wide bet (for example moving the org to secure defaults), with outcomes. One program delivered is Senior Manager depth | *"Drove a security-first culture"* | None shown |
| **Influence** | Shaped company planning and risk decisions; works with executives; led cross-org initiatives | Influence within own org only | None shown |
| **People** | Built a leadership bench; manages managers; succession; engagement and attrition data; org design | Hiring counts only | None |
| **Business impact** | Org outcomes tied to company goals: risk reduced with evidence, deals unblocked by engineering work, budget figures | A list of programs delivered | No outcomes. Coordinating audits or answering questionnaires is governance work: out of scope, set aside (see README) |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. Depth may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Delivery bullets that read like a Senior Manager.
- No org size or budget.
- No strategy or org design.
- No evidence of developing leaders.
- Impact described as tools bought or programs launched, not risk changed.
- Startup "Head of Security" with Manager scope.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Led security engineering. | Led security engineering (45 engineers, 5 teams, 5 managers, $9M budget including tooling and external testing); set the 2-year strategy that moved security from manual reviews to secure defaults built into the platform. |
| Improved security posture. | Reorganised the org from tool-based teams into teams aligned to product areas; median time to fix critical findings fell from 30 days to 6, and developer satisfaction with security support rose 20 points. |
| Worked with executives. | Partnered with the CTO and sales leadership on enterprise security; cut customer security review turnaround from 3 weeks to 3 days and passed 2 external audits with no major findings, unblocking about $15M in deals. |

## What holds people back at Director
- Impact not tied to company goals or to a change in risk.
- No org size, or no budget figures when they do own a budget. Not every company gives Directors a budget.
- Reads like a Senior Manager with a bigger team.
- No evidence of strategy or org design decisions.
- The resume reads as governance and compliance leadership with no engineering org behind it.
