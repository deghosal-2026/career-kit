# Security engineering Leader: Senior Manager

## In one line
Leads several security teams, often through managers or tech leads, roughly 15 to 40 people. Sets charters, plans headcount, and delivers security programs across teams.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Several teams with structure stated. *"Led 3 teams (24 engineers, 3 managers) covering product security, detection, and response."* | One large team, no structure | One team |
| **Ambiguity** | Defined team charters; reorganised teams around the top risks; turned security strategy into a multi-team roadmap | Managed teams with charters already set | No shaping |
| **Depth** | Arbitrated cross-team technical and risk trade-offs; set security architecture direction with Staff engineers | Team-level technical calls | No technical credibility shown |
| **Influence** | Shaped engineering and security planning; partnered with peer engineering leaders; changed priorities across the org | Influence within own teams only | None shown |
| **People** | Grew managers (first-time managers developed); built the hiring pipeline; planned succession | Hired ICs only | None |
| **Business impact** | Outcomes across teams tied to risk or business goals; owned a slice of headcount or budget | Team-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Ambiguity ideally Strong. Depth and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a one-team manager scaled up.
- No managers or tech leads reporting to them.
- No planning, headcount, or hiring plans.
- Outcomes listed as programs, not results.
- Work described as running audits or questionnaires rather than leading engineering teams.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed multiple security teams. | Led 3 teams (24 engineers, 3 managers) across product security, detection, and response; set team charters and a shared roadmap built around the company's top 5 risks. |
| Hired a lot of engineers. | Built the hiring plan for 10 roles in a year and filled 9; developed 2 senior engineers into first-time managers. |
| Rolled out stronger sign-in. | Delivered phishing-resistant sign-in for all 3,000 staff across 3 teams in 2 quarters; account takeover incidents fell from 6 a year to none. |

## What separates Senior Manager from Director
A Director resume shows:
- An org of several teams and managers, often 25 to 100+ people.
- The org's security strategy and structure, not only its delivery.
- Budget ownership, where the company gives Directors a budget, often including tooling and external testing.
- A leadership bench: managers of managers, succession.
- Partnership with executives, including on which risks to accept.
- Outcomes tied to company goals.
