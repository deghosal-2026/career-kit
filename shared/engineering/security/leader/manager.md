# Security engineering Leader: Manager

## In one line
Leads one security team, roughly 4 to 10 engineers. Accountable for the team's delivery, health, and growth, and credible on technical and risk calls.

Tech lead managers (a small team plus hands-on technical leadership) fit between tracks. If the resume could be judged either way, ask which level the user is aiming for and judge against that one.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Team size and charter stated, plus what the team covers. *"Managed 6 engineers covering application security for 400 developers."* | *"Led a team"* with no size or charter | Only IC bullets |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the team roadmap from the org's top risks and business goals; negotiated priorities with engineering and product | Delivered a roadmap handed down | No sign of shaping the work |
| **Depth** | Made or guided key technical and risk calls; set severity, fix-time, and review standards; credible in incident and design reviews | A list of tools and certifications | No technical credibility shown. A concern for a security engineering manager |
| **Influence** | Partner teams changed plans because of them; negotiated fix timelines; represented security risk in planning | *"Worked closely with engineering"* | Own team only |
| **People** | Hires, promotions, performance handled, retention; grew the team from X to Y | *"Mentored the team"* | None. A blocker for Manager |
| **Business impact** | Team outcomes with numbers: time to fix or contain, coverage, incidents, audit or customer review results; team health such as attrition or on-call load | Lists programs delivered | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Depth, Ambiguity, and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Bullets describe their own reviews or incident work, not the team's results.
- No team size anywhere.
- "I found" everywhere, "the team" nowhere.
- No hiring or growing people.
- Impact shown only as counts of findings or releases blocked.
- Manager title, but the evidence shows tech lead scope without reports.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the application security team. | Managed 6 engineers covering application security for 400 developers; grew the team from 3 and hired 4, with no regretted attrition in 2 years. |
| Improved incident response. | Set up the team's on-call rotation, runbooks, and quarterly response exercises; median time to contain fell from 2 days to 5 hours, and pages per engineer per week halved. |
| Mentored engineers. | Promoted 3 engineers (2 to Senior, 1 to Staff) through written growth plans and stretch work such as leading incident reviews. |

## What separates Manager from Senior Manager
A Senior Manager resume shows:
- Several teams, or managers and tech leads reporting to them, often across specialisms (for example product security plus detection and response).
- Charters set for several teams.
- Headcount planning and hiring plans.
- Cross-team security programs delivered.
- Growing managers and tech leads.
- Outcomes across teams, not one team.
