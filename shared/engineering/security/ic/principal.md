# Security engineering IC: Principal

Top of the IC track in career-kit. Levels above Principal are out of scope.

The Staff shapes (tech lead, architect, solver, right hand; see `staff.md`) apply here too, at org or company scale.

## In one line
Owns security direction for a domain across the org or company over years. Makes the risk consequences of technical choices clear to leadership, shapes their decisions, and is a recognised authority inside and outside the company.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | A security domain across the org or company. *"Set the access model every internal service now uses."* | Several teams within one org | One area or team |
| **Ambiguity** | Set multi-year security strategy that anticipates threats and growth; chose between steady improvement and a major redesign | Executed someone else's strategy at large scale | Delivers defined programs |
| **Depth** | Recognised authority: the person the company turns to for the domain; final reviewer for the most critical designs; owns standards others must follow. External signals (talks, published research, industry working groups) strengthen the case but are not required | Deep, but recognised only inside one area | No authority signal |
| **Influence** | Changed leadership decisions: security investment, risk acceptance, build vs buy, product direction; works with executives | Influence across teams within one org | Own area only |
| **People** | Grew Staff engineers; shaped the security career ladder, hiring bar, or how engineering and security work together | Mentors Seniors | None |
| **Business impact** | Company-level outcomes: risk profile changed, new markets or customers made possible, major cost or exposure removed | Org-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. People may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a strong Staff engineer with a long tenure.
- Outcomes confined to one org.
- No evidence of executive-level decisions.
- A long list of programs instead of a few big bets.
- Risk talked about in general terms, never tied to a decision or a measure.
- External visibility claimed but not shown (no talk, paper, or group named).

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Security architect for the company. | Set the 3-year plan to replace network-location trust with per-request identity checks across the company; the CTO's staff funded it, and 90% of internal services now enforce it. |
| Spoke at security conferences. | Gave 3 talks at major industry conferences on detecting abuse of build systems; the published method from that work has been adopted by several peer organisations. |
| Advised leadership on risk. | Built the company's first forecast of top security risks, with ranges for likelihood and cost; the exec team used it to move $3M of planned spend to identity and recovery work. |

## What holds people back at Principal
- No evidence of company-level decisions.
- Impact confined to one org.
- Too many small programs, not enough big bets.
- Authority claimed but not shown: no named decisions, reviews, or standards they own.
- The resume never says why the work mattered to the business, or what risk changed because of it.
