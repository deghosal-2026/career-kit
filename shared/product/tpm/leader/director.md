# TPM Leader: Director

Top of the Leader track in career-kit. VP and above are out of scope.

## In one line
Leads a TPM org of several managers, often 15 to 50+ people, or the whole TPM function at a smaller company. Owns how the org or company plans and delivers, sets TPM strategy and structure, and partners with executives.

Team size here is smaller than the engineering Director size in `levels.md`. A TPM org is small relative to the engineering org it serves; also credit the size of the engineering org the TPM org covers.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Org size and structure stated, plus coverage and budget where they own one. *"35 TPMs across 4 teams and 4 managers, covering a 600-engineer division."* | Several teams, no managers or org shape | One or two teams |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the TPM org's strategy; designed the company's planning or launch operating model; built the function from scratch or reshaped it | Runs an operating model set by others | No shaping |
| **Depth** | Set the bar for technical fluency and program quality in TPM hiring and reviews; made the call on the company's riskiest launches; major portfolio bets with outcomes | *"Drove execution excellence"* | None shown |
| **Influence** | Shaped company planning; works with executives on what to fund and sequence; led cross-org initiatives | Influence within own org only | None shown |
| **People** | Built a leadership bench; manages managers; succession; engagement and attrition data; designed the TPM career ladder or org | Hiring counts only | None |
| **Business impact** | Org or company outcomes tied to company goals: planning accuracy, time to market, launches that opened markets, risk and cost removed; budget or headcount figures | A list of programs delivered | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. Depth may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Delivery bullets that read like a Senior Manager.
- No org size, coverage, or budget.
- No operating model, strategy, or org design.
- No evidence of developing leaders.
- Impact described as programs shipped.
- Startup "Head of TPM" with Manager scope.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Led technical program management. | Led 35 TPMs (4 managers) covering a 600-engineer division; set the TPM strategy that moved TPMs from one central pool into teams aligned to the division's 4 product lines. |
| Improved company planning. | Designed the company's twice-yearly planning process across 9 orgs; cross-org dependency slips fell from 40% to 15%, and planning took 3 weeks instead of 7. |
| Worked with executives on launches. | Partnered with the CTO and finance on launch readiness for the company's 3 largest launches of the year; all hit their dates, and the readiness reviews caught a capacity gap that would have cost an estimated $2M in lost orders. |

## What holds people back at Director
- Impact not tied to company goals.
- No org size, or no budget figures when they do own a budget. Not every company gives TPM Directors a budget.
- Reads like a Senior Manager with a bigger team.
- No evidence of operating model, strategy, or org design decisions.
- TPM org described as a service desk for status reports, not a function that changes how the company delivers.
