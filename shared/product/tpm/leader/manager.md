# TPM Leader: Manager

## In one line
Leads one team of TPMs, roughly 3 to 8 people, covering the programs of a product or engineering area. Accountable for the team's delivery, health, and growth, and often still runs a program.

Team size here is smaller than the engineering Manager size in `levels.md`. TPM teams are small relative to the engineering orgs they serve; also credit the number of engineering teams the team's programs cover.

Player-coach managers (a small team plus a large program of their own) are judged as managers; don't mark them down for running a program. Use Hybrid only if their own program work dominates and reports are few (see the TPM README).

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Team size and coverage stated. *"Managed 5 TPMs covering programs for 12 engineering teams in commerce."* | *"Led a program team"* with no size or coverage | Only IC program bullets |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the team's charter and decided which programs got a TPM; turned area goals into a program plan | Staffed programs handed down | No sign of shaping the work |
| **Depth** | Credible in technical reviews; caught risks their TPMs missed; set the bar for program plans, risk reviews, and launch readiness **in one area** | A list of methods and tools | No technical credibility shown. A concern for a TPM manager |
| **Influence** | Engineering and product leaders changed plans because of them; represented the area in planning | *"Worked closely with engineering leadership"* | Own team only |
| **People** | Hires, promotions, performance handled, retention; grew the team from X to Y | *"Mentored the team"* | None. A blocker for Manager |
| **Business impact** | Team outcomes with numbers: delivery predictability across the area, launches landed, slips or incidents reduced; team health such as attrition or engagement | Lists programs delivered | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Depth, Ambiguity, and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Bullets describe their own programs, not the team's results.
- No team size anywhere.
- "I ran" everywhere, "my team" nowhere.
- No hiring or growing people.
- Manager title, but the evidence shows a senior TPM without reports.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the TPM team for commerce. | Managed 5 TPMs covering 12 engineering teams in commerce; grew the team from 2 and hired 3, with no regretted attrition in 2 years. |
| Improved delivery across the area. | Introduced a shared quarterly plan and a weekly cross-team risk review; on-time delivery of committed programs rose from about 55% to 85%. |
| Developed TPMs. | Promoted 2 TPMs to Senior through written growth plans and stretch programs; one now owns the area's largest launch. |

## What separates Manager from Senior Manager
A Senior Manager resume shows:
- A larger TPM team, or TPM managers reporting to them.
- Charters and TPM coverage set for several areas.
- Headcount planning: where TPMs go and where they do not.
- A portfolio of programs across an org.
- Growing TPM managers and Staff TPMs.
- Outcomes across an org, not one area.
