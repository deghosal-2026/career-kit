# TPM IC: Mid

## In one line
Runs a defined program or workstream across a small number of engineering teams on their own. Told *what* to deliver, decides *how* to plan, track, and land it.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Owned a named program or workstream end to end, usually 2 to 4 teams. *"Ran the rollout of the new billing interface across 3 client teams."* | Names the program, but not what they owned. *"Supported the billing migration."* | Only meetings, status reports, or tickets; no program named |
| **Ambiguity** | Turned a defined goal into a plan with milestones, owners, and dependencies; re-planned when facts changed | *"Maintained the project plan"* | No sign of any decision they made |
| **Depth** | Explains a technical dependency or risk and its effect on the schedule. *"Found the schema change blocked 2 downstream services and resequenced the work."* | A list of tools and delivery methods with no account of what they did with them | Only a skills section, or no technical content anywhere |
| **Influence** | Improved a team practice others adopted: a release checklist, a risk log, a status format engineers actually use | *"Ran stand-ups and retrospectives"* | Nothing beyond their own program. Fine at Mid |
| **People** | Onboarded a new TPM; taught engineers the release process | *"Helped teammates"* | None. Fine at Mid |
| **Business impact** | A delivery outcome with a number: date hit, cycle time, defects or rollbacks at launch. *"Shipped on the committed date with zero rollbacks."* | An outcome without a number. *"Delivered successfully."* | No outcomes, only activity |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, Depth, and Business impact at least Weak, ideally Strong. Influence and People are a bonus.

## Anti-signals
These pull a Mid TPM resume down toward a junior or non-technical coordinator:
- "Coordinated meetings", "took notes", "maintained trackers", "responsible for status reporting".
- No system, dependency, or technical risk named anywhere.
- Engineering work claimed as their own with no sign of their program part.
- A skills section of delivery methods and tools with little evidence behind any of them.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the login migration project. | Ran the move of 4 client apps to the new sign-in service across 3 teams; built the cutover plan and rollback steps, and finished 2 weeks early with no sign-in outages. |
| Tracked dependencies for the release. | Mapped 23 cross-team dependencies for the spring release; caught a blocked database change 5 weeks out and resequenced 2 teams' work to keep the date. |
| Improved the release process. | Wrote a release readiness checklist now used by all 5 teams in the group; release-day defects fell from about 9 per release to 3. |

## What separates Mid from Senior
A Senior resume shows:
- A cross-team program owned end to end, from a loose goal to launch, not a workstream within it.
- Several teams, often 4 to 10, with real dependencies between them.
- Technical trade-offs pushed to a decision: scope cut, sequencing changed, a design risk raised and resolved.
- Launch ownership: readiness, rollout, rollback, and the result after launch.
- Risks managed ahead of time, not reported after they hit.
- Regular mentoring of newer TPMs.

Quick check: the verbs shift from *tracked, coordinated, ran* to *planned, decided, owned the launch*.
