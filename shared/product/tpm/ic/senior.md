# TPM IC: Senior

## In one line
Owns a cross-team technical program end to end, from a loose goal to launch and after. Drives the technical trade-offs that keep it on track and often runs more than one program at once.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Owns a whole program across several teams, often 4 to 10, or one critical program; may run 2 or 3 at once. *"Owned the data center exit program: 6 teams, 40 engineers, 120 services."* | Owns a workstream within a program someone else runs | One team's project only |
| **Ambiguity** | Took a goal and produced the program: phases, success measures, dependencies, plan. *"Asked to cut checkout errors before peak season; split the work into 3 phases across 5 teams."* | Ran a plan someone else wrote | No sign of shaping the work |
| **Depth** | Challenged a design or estimate and changed the plan: raised a capacity, migration, or data risk and got it resolved; explains the trade-off (scope vs date, speed vs safety) | Describes risks and dependencies only in schedule terms | Status and tracking only |
| **Influence** | Got teams with different priorities to commit to shared dates; their plan, risk review, or launch process was adopted across the program | *"Worked with stakeholders"* | Only own work. A concern at Senior |
| **People** | Mentors 1 to 3 TPMs or engineers with a visible result; interviews TPMs | *"Mentored juniors"* with no detail | None. A concern at Senior |
| **Business impact** | Program-level outcome tied to a metric: launch date hit on a critical path, time to market, risk avoided, cost or engineering time saved, quality at launch | Lists programs delivered, no outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, Depth, and Business impact Strong. Ambiguity, Influence, and People at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Only Mid verbs: *coordinated, tracked, supported* with no *owned, planned, decided*.
- No launch ownership: nothing about readiness, rollout, rollback, or results after launch.
- Risks described only after they caused a slip.
- Every bullet is a status or reporting task.
- Program size missing: no teams, engineers, or systems counted.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the data center migration. | Owned the data center exit across 6 teams and 120 services; set the move order by risk and dependency, and finished 1 month ahead of the lease end, avoiding $1.8M in renewal costs. |
| Handled risks for the payments launch. | Found in design review that the fraud check would add 400 ms to checkout; pushed the teams to an async design, and the launch held its date with no conversion drop. |
| Ran launches for the group. | Owned launch readiness for 3 programs across 7 teams; added go and no-go reviews with rollback plans, and post-launch incidents fell from 5 a quarter to 1. |

## What separates Senior from Staff
A Staff resume shows:
- Several programs, a portfolio, or one program that spans orgs, not one program across teams.
- Problems nobody assigned: they found the cross-team gap, framed it, and got it funded.
- Program and process standards others follow: planning, launch, or risk practices adopted across an org.
- Influence without authority at senior levels: aligning directors and leaders with conflicting goals.
- Growing Senior TPMs.
- Outcomes tied to org goals, not one program's dates.

Trap: a Senior TPM running more programs is not Staff. More of the same work at bigger volume does not change the level.

Quick check: the verbs shift from *owned, planned, launched* to *framed, set the approach, aligned across orgs*.
