# TPM IC: Principal

Top of the IC track in career-kit. Levels above Principal are out of scope.

The Staff shapes (program lead, portfolio owner, operating model builder, right hand; see `staff.md`) apply here too, at company scale.

## In one line
Turns company strategy into multi-year programs across the company and runs the most complex of them. Shapes what leadership funds, stops, and sequences, and is the person the company turns to when a critical program is in trouble.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Company-wide programs across many orgs and functions. *"Ran the company's move to a new identity platform: 40 teams, 6 orgs, legal and support."* | Several programs within one org | One program or one org's portfolio |
| **Ambiguity** | Turned a company goal into a multi-year program with phases and success measures; reframed or stopped programs that would not reach the goal | Ran a company-level program someone else framed | Delivers defined programs |
| **Depth** | Recognised authority on running large technical programs: the person leaders send to rescue a failing one; final reviewer of launch readiness for the riskiest work; can test the technical basis of a plan across many domains | Strong program judgment, recognised only inside one org | No authority signal |
| **Influence** | Changed leadership decisions: funding, sequencing, build vs buy, whether to launch; works directly with executives; resolved disagreements across functions | Influence across teams within one org | Own programs only |
| **People** | Grew Staff TPMs; shaped the TPM career ladder, hiring bar, or how the company runs programs | Mentors Senior TPMs | None |
| **Business impact** | Company-level outcomes: a launch that opened a market, a regulatory deadline met, a major risk removed, a step change in delivery speed or cost | Org-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. People may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a strong Staff TPM with a long tenure.
- Outcomes confined to one org.
- No evidence of executive-level decisions.
- A long list of programs instead of a few company-level ones.
- No work beyond engineering, at a level where programs usually cross legal, finance, or operations.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Led the identity program. | Set the 2-year plan for moving all products to one identity platform across 40 teams and 6 orgs; the exec team funded it as written, and it retired 3 legacy systems costing $3.2M a year. |
| Managed the compliance program. | Ran the company's program to meet a new data protection law across 25 teams, legal, and support; met the deadline in all 4 markets with no findings at the first audit. |
| Advised leadership on priorities. | Showed the exec team that 2 of its 7 company bets shared a critical dependency nobody owned; they re-sequenced the plan, and both launched in the same half instead of slipping a year. |

## What holds people back at Principal
- No evidence of company-level decisions.
- Impact confined to one org.
- Too many programs, not enough company-level bets.
- Authority claimed but not shown: no named rescues, readiness calls, or standards they own.
- The resume never says why the programs mattered to the business.
