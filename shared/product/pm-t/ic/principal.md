# PM-T IC: Principal

Top of the IC track in career-kit. Levels above Principal are out of scope.

The Staff shapes (area lead, platform, new bet, right hand; see `staff.md`) apply here too, at org or company scale. At some companies "Principal" is the first title above Senior; if the evidence shows one cross-team area rather than a company domain, use `staff.md`.

## In one line
Owns product direction for a technical domain across the org or company over years. Shapes executive decisions on markets, platforms, and pricing, and is the person leaders turn to for that domain.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | A domain across the org or company. *"Set direction for the developer platform used by every product line and 6,000 external partners."* | Several teams within one org | One area or product |
| **Ambiguity** | Set multi-year product strategy; reframed what the company should build or which market to enter | Executed someone else's strategy at large scale | Delivers a defined roadmap |
| **Depth** | Recognised authority on the domain's customers, market, and technical direction; their strategy docs set the frame for others; makes the hardest trade-offs (platform consolidation, pricing model changes, ecosystem rules). External signals (customer advisory boards, talks, standards work) strengthen the case but are not required | Deep, but recognised only inside one area | No authority signal |
| **Influence** | Changed leadership decisions: market entry, build vs buy vs partner, investment levels; works directly with executives | Influence across teams within one org | Own area only |
| **People** | Grew Staff-level PMs; shaped the PM career ladder, hiring bar, or product practices | Coaches Senior PMs | None |
| **Business impact** | Company-level outcomes: new revenue lines, revenue mix, market position, cost base | Org-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** all dimensions Strong. People may be Weak if Influence and Business impact are very strong.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a strong Staff or Senior PM with a long tenure.
- Outcomes confined to one product or one org.
- No evidence of executive-level decisions.
- A long list of launches instead of a few big bets.
- Authority or external visibility claimed but not shown (no decision, strategy, talk, or board named).

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Product strategy lead for the platform. | Wrote the 3-year strategy to open the company's internal platform to outside developers; the exec team adopted it, and partner-built integrations now drive 18% of new revenue. |
| Advised leadership on pricing. | Led the move from per-seat to usage-based pricing for the data products; modelled it with finance, piloted with 40 accounts, and grew average contract value 26% with no rise in churn. |
| Worked with customers on the roadmap. | Started and ran a customer advisory board of 12 enterprise platform buyers; its input reshaped 2 years of roadmap and led the decision to partner, not build, for on-premises deployment. |

## What holds people back at Principal
- No evidence of company-level decisions.
- Impact confined to one product or one org.
- Too many launches, not enough big bets.
- Authority claimed but not shown: no named strategy, decision, or pricing change they owned.
- Technical depth shown, but the resume never says why the work mattered to customers or the business.
