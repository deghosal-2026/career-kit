# PM-T IC: Staff

Many companies have no Staff PM title. People at this level often hold Senior or Principal titles. Place them by evidence.

## In one line
Sets product direction for an area spanning several teams or products, or owns the hardest product bet in the org. Finds and frames problems nobody assigned, and aligns PMs and engineering teams who do not report to them.

## Shapes of Staff
Staff PM work comes in several common shapes. All are valid. Judge each by its own evidence; do not mark someone down for not being a different shape.

| Shape | What it looks like | Where Scope comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Area lead** | Owns strategy for a product area built by several teams, often with other PMs working in it | Number of teams and the area's importance |
| **Platform** | Owns a shared technical capability that many product teams or partners build on | How many teams, products, or customers depend on it |
| **New bet** | Takes an unproven product from idea to first customers and early revenue | The size of the opportunity and the risk taken |
| **Right hand** | Extends a product leader's reach: cross-org strategy, pricing, or planning work | The leader's org |

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | An area across several teams or products. *"Owned identity and access across 4 product lines, built by 5 engineering teams."* | One large product with one or two teams | One product's features |
| **Ambiguity** | Spotted an unassigned problem or opportunity, built the case, and got it funded or prioritised | Led a cross-team product effort someone else defined | Executes an assigned roadmap |
| **Depth** | Wrote product strategy or platform principles that teams follow; makes technical trade-offs that shape several products (shared data model, one platform vs many, deprecation policy) | Strong judgment within one product only | No strategy authorship |
| **Influence** | Several teams or PMs changed roadmaps because of them; aligned teams with conflicting goals; trusted by engineering leaders | *"Partnered with other teams"* | Only own product. A blocker for Staff |
| **People** | Grew Senior PMs (promotions); set PM practices others use (strategy templates, review forums); raises the PM hiring bar | Coaches newer PMs only | None |
| **Business impact** | Org-level outcomes: revenue across products, platform adoption across teams, cost removed, a new line of business started | One product's metric | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, Ambiguity, and Influence Strong. For the New bet shape, Scope can be Strong through the size and risk of the bet even when few teams are involved. Depth and Business impact Strong, or Weak with a clear story. People at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Every bullet sits inside one product's roadmap.
- Cross-team work described with *helped, supported, contributed to*.
- No written strategy: no strategy docs, principles, or pricing proposals.
- Outcomes measured only at feature or single-product level.
- Reads like a program manager: dependencies and timelines across teams, but no product decision.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Worked with several teams on single sign-on. | Proposed one identity platform to replace 4 separate login systems across product lines; wrote the strategy, aligned 5 engineering teams and 3 PMs, and enterprise deals blocked on access controls fell from 22 a quarter to 4. |
| Owned the internal data platform roadmap. | Found that 9 product teams were each building their own event pipelines; built the case for a shared ingestion product, got it funded, and reached adoption by 7 teams in 3 quarters, removing about $1.1M a year in duplicate spend. |
| Mentored PMs on the team. | Coached 3 Senior PMs, 2 since promoted; started the monthly product strategy review that every new platform proposal now goes through. |

## What separates Staff from Principal
A Principal resume shows:
- A product domain across the org or company, not one area.
- Multi-year product direction, not quarter-to-quarter roadmaps.
- Influence on leadership decisions: which markets to enter, platform bets, build vs buy vs partner, pricing models.
- Recognition beyond their own area and often outside the company: customer advisory boards, industry talks, standards bodies.
- Growing Staff-level PMs.
- Outcomes that change the company's revenue mix, market position, or cost base.
