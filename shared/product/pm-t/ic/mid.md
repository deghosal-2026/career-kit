# PM-T IC: Mid

## In one line
Owns features or a focused part of a technical product with one engineering team. The area and goals are set by others; decides *which* problems to solve within it and *how* the solution should work for users.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Owned named features end to end, from problem to launch to measured result. *"Owned webhook retries and delivery logs for the payments API."* | Names the product, but not what they owned. *"PM on the developer platform."* | Only tasks: backlog grooming, writing tickets |
| **Ambiguity** | Turned a defined goal into a scoped feature; chose what to cut and why | *"Wrote requirements for features assigned by my manager"* | No sign of any decision they made |
| **Depth** | Talked to users and used data to shape the feature; made a clear technical trade-off with engineering (scope vs latency, new endpoint vs extending one) and can say why | Lists methods or technologies with no decision behind them | Only a skills section |
| **Influence** | Got design, engineering, and support aligned on a feature; a doc or process they made was reused by the team | *"Worked closely with engineering"* | Nothing beyond their own work. Fine at Mid |
| **People** | Onboarded a new PM or helped an engineer understand customers | *"Helped the team"* | None. Fine at Mid |
| **Business impact** | An outcome with a number: adoption, activation, errors, tickets, revenue. *"Cut integration support tickets by 35%."* | An outcome without a number. *"Improved developer experience."* | No outcomes, only launches |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, Depth, and Business impact at least Weak, ideally Strong. Influence and People are a bonus.

## Anti-signals
These pull a Mid resume down toward associate or junior:
- "Assisted with", "supported", "responsible for", "exposure to".
- Bullets about writing tickets, running standups, or managing the backlog, with no problem or outcome.
- Every bullet ends at "launched"; nothing about what changed after.
- Engineering bullets from a past role written as if they were product work, with no customer or user in sight.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Responsible for the API documentation experience. | Found from 20 developer interviews that new users stalled at authentication; shipped a guided quick-start and sample apps, cutting median time to first successful call from 3 days to 40 minutes. |
| Worked on webhooks for the payments product. | Owned webhook retries and delivery logs for the payments API; chose a fixed retry schedule over a configurable one to ship a quarter earlier, and failed-delivery tickets fell 35%. |
| Launched a new dashboard feature. | Launched usage alerts in the data platform's console; 28% of paying accounts set one in the first month, and surprise-bill complaints dropped by half. |

## What separates Mid from Senior
A Senior resume shows:
- Ownership of a whole product or product area (an API, a platform capability, a developer product), not features within it.
- A roadmap they set, from customer research and data, not one handed to them.
- Technical trade-offs that shaped the product's direction, not one feature.
- Work across 1 or 2 engineering teams and partner functions such as sales, support, or developer relations.
- Outcomes tied to the product's metrics: adoption, retention, revenue, cost to serve.
- Coaching newer PMs or mentoring interns.

Quick check: the verbs shift from *shipped, launched, wrote* to *owned, set, decided*.
