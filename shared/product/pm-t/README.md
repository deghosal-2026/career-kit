# PM-T rubric (Product Manager, Technical)

Covers product managers who own outcomes for technical products or technically deep features: APIs, platforms, developer products, infrastructure products, data products, and complex technical features inside larger products. They find customer problems, set the roadmap, make technical trade-offs with engineering, and are accountable for adoption and business results.

## Scope and boundaries
- **In scope:** product and customer facing PMs whose product, users, or key decisions are technical. Customers may be external (developers, IT buyers, partners) or internal (product teams that build on a platform).
- **Not TPM.** Technical Program Managers are a separate career-kit discipline. TPMs are internal facing and own *delivery* across engineering teams: plans, dependencies, risks, launches. PM-Ts own *what* gets built and *why*, and are judged on product outcomes. A resume full of schedules, dependency tracking, and launch coordination with no customer problem, roadmap call, or adoption result reads as TPM. Judge with the files of the discipline the person is applying in; for a move between the two, see `utils/transitions/discipline-switch/`.
- **Not general non-technical PM.** Consumer, growth, or business PMs whose work has no meaningful technical depth are out of scope. If the person is a PM without technical products or technical trade-offs, say so and apply only the parts that still hold.

## Notes for the evaluator
- **Depth means two things here: product judgment and technical fluency.** Product judgment is customer insight, prioritisation, and strategy. Technical fluency is the ability to make and explain trade-offs with engineering (build vs reuse, API design and versioning, performance vs cost, migration paths). Strong evidence shows both. A resume with technical words but no product decision is Weak on Depth; so is one with product language but no technical trade-off.
- **PM-Ts do not write production code.** Do not reward a PM-T resume for coding. Former engineers often undersell their product work by writing engineering bullets; flag that as a Reframe.
- **Scope means the product area owned**, not team size. A PM-T usually works with one or more engineering teams but has no reports. Name the product, its users, and how many teams build it.
- **Business impact is adoption, revenue, retention, cost, and customer outcomes.** For technical products, valid metrics include active integrations, API call volume, developer activation and time to first success, platform adoption by internal teams, migration completion, support tickets per customer, and cost to serve. Internal platform PMs may never see revenue; internal adoption and cost are full credit.
- **Shipping is not an outcome.** "Launched X" is activity. Ask what changed for customers or the business. Do not push `[CONFIRM]` for a number on every line; ask where a number would change how the bullet reads.
- **The PM works through others.** Credit for outcomes is fine when the PM's part is clear: the problem they found, the call they made, the trade-off they negotiated. "We shipped" with no PM decision visible is Weak.
- **Titles vary more than in engineering.** Many companies go from Senior straight to Principal or to Group PM, with no Staff title. Some use "Lead PM" for Staff scope. Place the person by evidence, not title.
- **"Group Product Manager" can be IC or manager.** At most companies a Group PM manages PMs (use `leader/manager.md`). At some, it is a senior IC title for a person who owns a large area with no reports (use `ic/staff.md` or `ic/principal.md`). Check for reports, and if both signals are present, judge the dominant track and count the other side as supporting evidence.
- **Leader team sizes are smaller than in engineering.** A product org has far fewer PMs than the engineering org it partners with (often one PM for every 5 to 10 engineers). The leader files use PM team sizes, not the engineering sizes in `levels.md`. Credit leaders for the size of the engineering and design org they shape as well as their PM reports, but judge people management on the PMs they manage.
- **Years of experience are not a level signal.** Judge scope and impact.
- **Written communication counts heavily.** Strategy docs, product requirements, pricing proposals, and launch narratives are evidence under Depth and Influence from Senior up.

- **Reach decides the level, not the size of the number.** Apply the outside-observer test (`../../levels.md` §6–7): a Senior outcome gets repeated in a team or product review; a Staff outcome in an engineering-leadership meeting; a Principal or Director outcome in front of company leadership or on an external stage. Judge who benefits and how widely, not the percentage.
- **Levels are awarded conservatively.** Promotion lags demonstrated performance — the next level's scope must already be shown, consistently, over time. One level-sized project inside a year of lower-level work reads as the lower level with a highlight, not as the next level. See `../../levels.md` §8.
