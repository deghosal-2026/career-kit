# PM-T Leader: Senior Manager

## In one line
Leads several product groups, often through Group PMs or lead PMs, roughly 6 to 15 PMs. Sets strategy across groups, plans PM headcount, and owns outcomes across a product line.

PM teams are smaller than engineering teams. The 15 to 40 range in `levels.md` is an engineering figure. A Senior Manager of 10 PMs may shape the work of 60 or more engineers; credit both, but judge People on the PM org.

"Head of Product" at a small company may be a Manager, Senior Manager, or Director. Judge by PM reports and scope.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Several product groups with structure stated. *"Led 11 PMs, including 2 Group PMs, across the data platform products."* | One larger PM team, no structure | One small PM team |
| **Ambiguity** | Defined group charters; reorganised product areas around customers or goals; turned company strategy into a multi-group roadmap | Managed groups with charters already set | No shaping |
| **Depth** | Made the cross-group calls: shared platforms vs separate products, packaging, pricing, deprecations; sets the product bar for the org | Group-level product calls only | No product judgment shown |
| **Influence** | Shaped company planning; partnered with engineering, design, sales, and finance leaders; changed priorities across orgs | Influence within own groups only | None shown |
| **People** | Grew PMs into Group PMs or lead PMs; built the PM hiring pipeline; planned succession | Hired PMs only | None |
| **Business impact** | Outcomes across a product line tied to business goals: revenue, retention, adoption, cost; owned PM headcount or a slice of budget | Group-level outcomes | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Ambiguity ideally Strong. Depth and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Reads like a one-group Group PM scaled up.
- No Group PMs or lead PMs reporting to them, or no structure at all.
- No planning, headcount, or hiring plans.
- Outcomes listed as launches, not results.
- Strategy described in words only ("set the vision") with no decision or result.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Led product for the data platform. | Led 11 PMs (2 Group PMs) across ingestion, storage, and analytics products; set group charters and one roadmap tied to the company's move upmarket, and enterprise share of data revenue rose from 35% to 52%. |
| Built out the product team. | Planned and filled 6 PM roles in a year; developed 2 Senior PMs into first-time Group PMs, both still in role 2 years later. |
| Launched new pricing for the platform. | Replaced 3 product-by-product price lists with one usage-based plan across the data platform, built with finance and sales; expansion revenue grew 31% and discount approvals fell by half. |

## What separates Senior Manager from Director
A Director resume shows:
- A product org of several groups and PM managers, often 10 to 30 or more PMs, sometimes including design or analytics.
- The org's product strategy and structure, not only its roadmap.
- Budget or P&L ownership, where the company gives product Directors one.
- A leadership bench: managers of PM managers, succession.
- Partnership with executives on company strategy.
- Outcomes tied to company goals.
