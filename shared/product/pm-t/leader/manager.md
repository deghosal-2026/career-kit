# PM-T Leader: Manager

## In one line
Leads a small team of PMs, roughly 3 to 6, who own related technical products. Accountable for the group's product outcomes and the PMs' growth, and still owns product calls for the group.

This is often a player-coach role: the Group PM manages PMs and may also own a product directly. A Group PM with no reports is an IC; use `ic/staff.md` or `ic/principal.md`. When the resume shows both, judge the dominant track and count the other side as supporting evidence.

PM teams are smaller than engineering teams. The 4 to 10 range in `levels.md` is an engineering figure. Credit the size of the engineering and design teams the group works with, but judge People on the PMs managed.

## Evidence by dimension

| Dimension | Strong | Weak | Absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Team size and product area stated. *"Managed 4 PMs owning the payments APIs, working with 5 engineering teams."* | *"Led the product team"* with no size or area | Only IC product bullets |
| **Ambiguity** | Set the group's strategy and roadmap from company goals; decided where each PM's product fits | Ran a roadmap set by leaders above | No sign of shaping the work |
| **Depth** | Made or guided the group's key product and technical calls; reviews PMs' strategy and specs; sets the bar for customer research and metrics | Talks about process only (planning cadence, rituals) | No product judgment shown |
| **Influence** | Engineering, design, sales, or other groups changed plans because of them; represented the group in planning | *"Worked closely with engineering leadership"* | Own team only |
| **People** | Hired PMs, promoted PMs, handled performance, kept strong PMs; grew the team from X to Y | *"Mentored the PM team"* | None. A blocker for Manager |
| **Business impact** | Group outcomes with numbers: adoption, revenue, retention, cost; team health such as attrition or engagement | Lists launches across the group | No outcomes |

**Bar for a fit:** Scope, People, and Business impact Strong. Depth, Ambiguity, and Influence at least Weak.

## Anti-signals
- Bullets describe their own product work only, not the group's results.
- No team size or PM reports anywhere.
- "I launched" everywhere, "my PMs" nowhere.
- No hiring or growing PMs.
- Group PM title, but the evidence shows a senior IC owning a large area with no reports.

## Weak to Strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Managed the developer tools product team. | Managed 4 PMs owning the developer tools group (3 products, 5 engineering teams); hired 2 of them and set a shared activation goal that lifted 30-day developer retention from 38% to 51%. |
| Coached PMs on strategy. | Introduced written quarterly strategy reviews for each PM's product; promoted 2 PMs to Senior in 18 months, with no regretted attrition. |
| Owned the payments API roadmap. | Merged 3 separate payments API roadmaps into one plan ranked by revenue at risk; closed the 5 gaps blocking enterprise deals, adding $4M in annual contract value. |

## What separates Manager from Senior Manager
A Senior Manager resume shows:
- A larger PM team, often with Group PMs or lead PMs reporting to them.
- Strategy and charters set for several product groups.
- Headcount planning and PM hiring plans.
- Product decisions that cut across groups: shared platforms, pricing, packaging.
- Growing PM managers and lead PMs.
- Outcomes across product groups, not one group.
